The Journey of a Web Request

Click the button to follow your request step by step!

1
You type "google.com" and hit Enter

Your browser needs to find where Google lives.

2
DNS Lookup — The Internet's Address Book

DNS converts "google.com" into a number like 142.250.80.46 — the real address.

3
Your request travels through routers

Like mail sorting centers, routers pass your request from network to network.

4
It reaches Google's server

A powerful computer in a data center reads your request.

5
The server sends back the webpage

HTML, CSS, images — everything your browser needs to draw the page.

6
Your browser displays the page!

All of this happens in under 1 second. ⚡

Key Players of the Internet

These are the things that make the internet work. Click each one to learn more!

📶

Wi-Fi & Router

Your home's connection to the internet

🖥️

Server

A computer that serves websites to you

🏠

IP Address

Every device's home address on the internet

🏭

Data Center

Huge buildings full of servers

Wi-Fi & Router: Your router is like your home's mailbox. It connects all your devices (phone, laptop, tablet) to the internet. Wi-Fi is just the wireless signal that lets you connect without a cable.

Server: A server is just a computer — but instead of running games, it runs websites. When you visit a website, you're really asking someone else's computer to send you files. It "serves" them to you!

IP Address: Every device on the internet has a number, like a home address. For example, Google's IP address is 142.250.80.46. DNS is like a phone book that converts names (google.com) to numbers.

Data Center: Companies like Google and Netflix have enormous buildings filled with thousands of servers. They're kept cool with powerful AC systems because all those computers generate a LOT of heat!

Request & Response

The internet runs on a simple conversation pattern:

📱 Your Device
➡️
Request
➡️
🖥️ Server
🖥️ Server
➡️
Response
➡️
📱 Your Device

Every time you use the internet, this happens:

  • Request: "Hey server, can I see this webpage?"
  • Response: "Sure! Here are the files you need."

This back-and-forth can happen hundreds of times just to load a single page!

Anatomy of a URL

Every web address has parts, just like a real mailing address!

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=abc123

https://

Protocol — The language browsers and servers speak. "https" means it's secure (the 's' = safe!).

www.youtube.com

Domain — The website's name. Like the name on a mailbox.

/watch

Path — Which page on the website. Like the room number in a building.

?v=abc123

Parameters — Extra info. This tells YouTube which video to show.

The 3 Parts of Every App

Every app — Instagram, Google, Minecraft — is made of three layers:

🎨 Frontend

What you see and click

↕️

🧠 Backend

The brain that processes everything

↕️

💾 Database

The memory that stores everything

Frontend is everything you interact with: buttons, images, menus, animations. On your phone it's the app. On your computer it's the website. The frontend sends your clicks and taps to the backend.

Backend is the server — a computer somewhere that does the hard work. It processes your requests, applies rules, and figures out what to send back. Think of it as the kitchen in a restaurant!

Database is where all the data lives: your profile, your photos, your scores, your messages. Without the database, the app would forget everything every time you close it!
