Large = REALLY large. We're talking hundreds of billions of parameters (each parameter is like one m or b in y = mx + b). GPT-4 has over 1 trillion parameters. If you wrote one per second, it would take over 31,000 years!

Language = It works with human language — English, Spanish, code, emoji, you name it. It trained by reading billions of sentences from books, websites, and articles and learned the patterns of how humans write and speak.

Model = Remember from last week? A model is a simplified version of something real. This model is a simplified version of how language works — which words follow which, what makes a sentence make sense, how questions get answered.

Generative = It generates (creates) brand-new text. It doesn't look up a stored answer — it writes a new sentence word by word, every single time. That's what makes it "Gen AI"!

Pre-trained = Before you ever talked to it, it already did its homework. It read billions of pages of text and learned language patterns. "Pre" means it trained before being released — you're talking to a model that already graduated from the world's biggest school!

Transformer = A type of neural network that's really good at understanding context. When you write "I saw a bat at the baseball game," the Transformer knows "bat" means a wooden stick, not a flying animal — because it figures out which words in the sentence relate to each other.

Put it all together: A GPT is a neural network that generates text, was pre-trained on the internet, and uses a Transformer to understand context. A LLM is the broader category — ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are all LLMs!

Two Superpowers: Understanding vs. Generating

AI language models come in two flavors — just like your brain has two skills when someone talks to you.

📖

Language Understanding

Reading & Comprehension

Like when someone asks you "What is your name?" and your brain understands the question — it figures out the meaning, the intent, and what kind of answer is expected.

AI examples: Spam filters reading your email, Google understanding your search, autocorrect fixing your typos.

✍️

Language Generation

Writing & Creating

Like when your brain formulates the answer — "My name is Sarah" — choosing the right words, in the right order, to make a sentence that makes sense.

AI examples: ChatGPT writing essays, Claude answering questions, AI generating stories or code.

Your brain does both! When someone says "What is your name?" your brain first understands (that's a question about my identity) and then generates a response ("My name is Sarah"). LLMs like ChatGPT and Claude do both too — they understand your prompt, then generate a response one word at a time.

Understanding models are called autoencoding models. "Auto" = self, "encoding" = turning words into meaning. Example: BERT (made by Google).

Generating models are called autoregressive models. "Auto" = self, "regressive" = predicting what comes next based on what came before. Example: GPT (made by OpenAI).

Modern LLMs like ChatGPT and Claude combine both — they understand AND generate!

How an LLM Answers "What Is Your Name?"

Let's walk through exactly what happens inside the AI, step by step. Click each step!

1

Break Into Tokens

The AI breaks your sentence into small pieces called tokens. "What is your name?" becomes: ["What", " is", " your", " name", "?"]

Tokens are like puzzle pieces. Some are whole words, some are parts of words. The word "basketball" might become ["basket", "ball"]. The AI works with these pieces, not whole sentences.

2

Understand the Context

The Transformer looks at ALL the tokens together and figures out the meaning. "What" + "is" + "your" + "name" = someone is asking for my identity.

This is where "attention" kicks in. The AI asks: which words matter most for understanding this sentence? "What" and "name" are the most important — they tell the AI this is a question about identity. "Is" and "your" provide structure.

3

Predict the Next Word

The AI asks: "After all these tokens, what word is most likely to come next?" Answer: "My". Then it asks again: after "My," what's next? "name". Then "is". Then "Claude".

This is the autocomplete superpower! The AI doesn't "know" its name like you know yours. It learned from millions of conversations that after "What is your name?" the pattern is usually "My name is ___." It's predicting the most likely next word, every single time.

The secret: An LLM is just a next-word prediction machine. It generates entire essays, poems, and code by predicting one word at a time, billions of times. The "intelligence" comes from the patterns it learned during training.

What Is a Prompt? 🎬

Think of the AI like an actor who takes on any role you give them. Every time you chat with an LLM, two things happen behind the scenes — like a TV show being filmed!

🎦

The Director's Brief

= System Prompt

Before filming starts, the director tells the actor: "You are a sarcastic pirate chef who only speaks in rhymes." The actor doesn't break character — no matter what happens on set!

👀 The audience (you!) never sees this part.

🎤

Your Line

= User Prompt

Now YOU walk on set and say something: "Hey, what should I make for dinner tonight?" The actor responds — in full pirate chef rhyme mode, because that's their character!

✏️ This is the part YOU write every time.

Here's what it actually looks like behind the scenes:

SYSTEM PROMPT (hidden)

You are a friendly homework helper for 7th graders. Keep answers short, use simple words, and always end with an encouraging emoji.

USER PROMPT (you typed this)

What is photosynthesis?

AI RESPONSE

Photosynthesis is how plants make their own food! They use sunlight, water, and air to create energy — kind of like solar panels, but for plants. 🌱

Same user question — "What is photosynthesis?" — but with a different system prompt:

NEW SYSTEM PROMPT

You are a university biology professor. Use technical terminology.

AI RESPONSE NOW

Photosynthesis is the biochemical process by which photoautotrophs convert light energy into chemical energy, stored as glucose via the Calvin cycle...

Same AI. Same question. Completely different vibe! The director changed, so the actor changed.

Why "You Are a..." Changes Everything

Watch what happens when we ask the same question but change just the first line of the prompt:

PROMPT A

You are a doctor.

Why does my head hurt?

AI ANSWER

"Headaches can be caused by dehydration, stress, lack of sleep, or eye strain. Try drinking water and resting. If it persists, see a healthcare provider."

PROMPT B

You are a computer expert.

Why does my head hurt?

AI ANSWER

"You might be getting screen fatigue! Try the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Also check your monitor brightness and posture."

Same question. Totally different answers! The "You are a..." instruction changes which patterns the LLM uses. When it's told "you are a doctor," it pulls from medical language patterns. When told "you are a computer expert," it pulls from tech patterns. You're steering which part of its training it uses!

Remember: the LLM predicts the most likely next word. When you say "You are a doctor," the AI thinks: "In all the text I've read, when a doctor is asked about headaches, they usually talk about dehydration, stress, and medical advice." The role changes which patterns are most likely — and that changes the entire answer!

Pro tip: Be specific! "You are a pediatric doctor who explains things to kids" will give even more tailored answers than just "You are a doctor."

Prompt Playground: Try These!

Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini and try each of these prompts. Notice how the role changes the answer!

1

The Homework Helper

"You are a friendly 7th grade math teacher. Explain what a variable is using a pizza analogy."

Now try the same question WITHOUT the role: just ask "Explain what a variable is." Compare the two answers. Which was more fun to read? Which helped you understand better?

2

The Story Writer

"You are a creative author who writes funny adventure stories for teenagers. Write a short story about a kid whose backpack secretly contains a portal to another dimension."

Now change the role to "You are a serious news reporter" and ask the same thing. The AI will write about the backpack as a news article! Same topic, completely different style.

3

The Debate Coach

"You are a debate coach. Give me 3 arguments FOR and 3 arguments AGAINST homework. Keep each argument to one sentence."

Notice how the prompt asked for a specific format (3 + 3, one sentence each). Being specific about what you want helps the AI give you exactly what you need!

4

The Translator

"You are a translator who also explains cultural context. Translate 'It's raining cats and dogs' into Spanish, and explain why a direct translation would confuse a Spanish speaker."

This prompt does two things: asks for a translation AND an explanation. Layering your requests helps you get richer, more interesting answers!

The pattern: Great prompts have three parts: (1) Who the AI should be, (2) What you want it to do, and (3) How you want the answer formatted. The more specific you are, the better the result!

Wrap-Up & Homework

"An LLM is a giant neural network that predicts the next word. A great prompt tells it WHO to be, WHAT to do, and HOW to answer."

What we learned today:

  • LLM = Large Language Model — a huge neural network trained on language
  • GPT = Generative Pre-trained Transformer — one type of LLM
  • AI has two superpowers: understanding language and generating language
  • System prompt = hidden role instructions; User prompt = your message
  • "You are a ___" steers which patterns the AI uses to respond

Build Your Own Prompt

Write a prompt that uses all three parts:

1. Who: "You are a ___"
2. What: the task or question
3. How: format instructions

Example: "You are a sports commentator. Describe my morning routine as if it's an exciting game. Use play-by-play style."
