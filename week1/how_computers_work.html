Face ID, QR codes, photos

Your phone's accelerometer is also an input device! When you tilt your phone to steer in a racing game, the computer inside is reading the angle of the tilt. That's input!

Ask: "When you play a video game, how many different kinds of input are you giving at the same time?" (Controller buttons, joystick, voice chat, maybe even a camera for VR.)

Memory — The Digital Filing Cabinet

A computer needs to remember things. It has two kinds of memory:

💭

Short-Term Memory (RAM)

What the computer is thinking about right now. Fast, but forgets everything when you turn it off.

Like the thoughts in your head during a test.

💾

Long-Term Memory (Storage)

Where files, photos, apps, and games are saved permanently. Slower, but keeps everything even when off.

Like everything you've learned in school over the years.

A typical phone has about 6 GB of RAM (short-term) and 128 GB of storage (long-term).

For comparison, 128 GB could hold about 32,000 songs or 40 hours of HD video!

Analogy: RAM is like your desk — it's where you spread out the stuff you're working on right now. Storage is like your closet — everything is packed away but you can grab it when you need it.

The Processor (CPU) — The Brain

The CPU is where the real "thinking" happens. It reads instructions and does calculations incredibly fast.

⚡

How fast? Let's compare:

🧑
1
math problem per second
(for most of us!)
💻
5,000,000,000
operations per second
(that's 5 BILLION!)

The CPU follows a simple cycle, billions of times per second: Fetch an instruction → Decode it → Execute it → Repeat.

The CPU can only do simple things (add, compare, move data) — but it does them SO fast that complex things emerge. This is a good foreshadowing for AI: simple rules + speed = seemingly intelligent behavior.
7 / 10

Output — Showing the Results

After the CPU does its work, the computer needs to show us the results. That's output!

📺

Screen / Display

Everything you see — websites, games, videos

🔊

Speakers

Music, sound effects, voice assistants talking back

🖨️

Printer

Turning digital into paper

🤖

Motors / Robots

Physical movement controlled by code

When you play a video game, your screen refreshes 60 times per second (or more!). Each frame is a brand new image the computer calculated and drew. That's 60 outputs per second just for what you see — plus sound, controller vibration, and more!

Ask: "What's the output when you ask Siri a question?" (Voice through the speaker AND text on the screen — two outputs at once!)
